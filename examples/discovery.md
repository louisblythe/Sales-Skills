# Worked example: discovery call notes

## Input

These notes are fictional.

> Ops reconciles Salesforce and a spreadsheet for two hours each Friday.
> Finance spotted discrepancies last month. The ops lead said a pilot
> “might be possible next quarter.”

Ask your agent to use the discovery skill to write a brief with confirmed
needs, open questions and a 20-minute follow-up agenda.

## Example output

### Confirmed observations

- Ops spends about two hours each Friday reconciling Salesforce and a spreadsheet.
- Finance spotted discrepancies last month. The notes give neither their cause nor their impact.
- The ops lead mentioned a possible pilot next quarter. No pilot date or approval was confirmed.

### Questions to resolve

- Which fields disagree, and what does Finance do when they disagree?
- Does the two-hour estimate cover the full process, including corrections?
- Who owns the budget and who would approve a pilot?
- What result would make a pilot worthwhile? Could the team measure reconciliation time and count discrepancies before and after?

### Proposed follow-up agenda

| Time | Topic |
| --- | --- |
| 5 min | Walk through one recent reconciliation. |
| 7 min | Trace a discrepancy and identify where the data diverged. |
| 5 min | Agree on a baseline and discuss possible pilot success criteria. |
| 3 min | Identify the decision owner and agree on the next step. |

The follow-up tests whether the buyer has an actionable problem. It leaves
budget, decision authority and timeline open until the buyer confirms them.
